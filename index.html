Abstract

Rapid progress in AI agents and robotics makes it a matter of the near future that humans are served by a team of robots. Such a team will receive several goals at once, each specified in whatever form is convenient for the human, such as a category name, a photo, or a spoken description. Existing benchmarks study multi-agent, multi-modal, and multi-object goal navigation in isolation, and a benchmark that places all three in one episode is missing. To fill this gap, we introduce HM3D-M³.

A team of K agents starts at distributed locations in a photorealistic indoor scene and must find an unordered set of M object goals, each given as an open-vocabulary category, an instance image, or an instance description. HM3D-M³ provides 1,000 validation episodes over 3,926 goals from 379 open-vocabulary categories, 100K training samples, and two team-level metrics, goal-level SR and makespan-normalized SPL. We further contribute two baselines. HiGA is a trained hierarchical goal assigner on top of a frozen open-vocabulary navigation policy, and LaViRA-M³ is a training-free team of VLM agents with shared mapping and shared progress. Adapted multi-robot and zero-shot methods reach 16 to 27% SR, while our baselines reach 38 to 41% with complementary strengths and orthogonal failure modes. Furthermore, we deploy LaViRA-M³ on a heterogeneous three-robot team.

Multi-Agent, Multi-Modal, and Multi-Object

Multi-agent: a team of K ∈ {2, 3, 4} agents starts at distributed, jointly reachable locations and shares a map; a goal counts as found as soon as any team member declares it within 1 m of the instance. Multi-object: each episode carries M ∈ {3, 4, 5} unordered goals, all known at the start. Multi-modal: every goal is individually specified as an open-vocabulary category (ON), an instance image (IIN), or an instance-level textual description (TN) — IIN and TN point to a specific instance, while ON accepts any instance of the category.
Benchmark statistics: 1,000 validation episodes on 36 held-out scenes, with 3,926 goals from 379 open-vocabulary categories (1,299 ON / 1,313 TN / 1,314 IIN): episodes per (K, M) cell (a), goals per modality for each M (b), goal spread — the mean pairwise geodesic distance between goals — against the shortest team tour d*, correlated with r = 0.71 (c), and the number of same-category instances sharing the floor with each IIN and TN goal (d). 100K training samples on disjoint scenes are provided for learned methods.

Baselines

We contribute two baselines representing two paradigms: 1) HiGA, a trained hierarchical goal assigner — a transformer over agent, goal, and map tokens assigns goals to agents, and each agent executes its assignment with a frozen open-vocabulary navigation policy; and 2) LaViRA-M³, a training-free team of VLM agents in which every agent runs a language–vision–robot action agent and the team shares a top-down map, a goal board, and natural-language progress analysis — no goal is ever explicitly assigned.

Adapted methods find only 16–27% of the goals, while our two baselines reach 38–41% with complementary strengths: HiGA is the strongest on category goals (53.0% ON) but weak on image goals (18.8% IIN) and the least efficient (7.1% SPL), whereas LaViRA-M³ is the strongest on instance goals (40.9% TN, 41.4% IIN) and the most efficient (20.3% SPL). Neither paradigm dominates.

Qualitative and failure analysis

Left: a K = 4, M = 3 episode of LaViRA-M³ with one goal per modality, agents in different colors. Right: where missed goals go, as a fraction of all goals, for (a) LaViRA-M³ and (b) HiGA.
Failure attribution: per-goal analysis attributes every missed goal to one cause, and the two baselines fail in orthogonal ways. LaViRA-M³ misses 59.0% of all goals, mostly at claim time — 41.8% are declared FOUND from more than 1 m away (pose miss) and 8.9% are rejected by its own verification, while only 8.3% are never claimed. HiGA misses 62.1%, mostly in perception — in 25.7% the detector fires but the approach does not end within 1 m of the right instance, 11.9% (all image goals) fail keypoint confirmation, and 16.4% pass within 3 m undetected. The two profiles are complementary.

Real-World Deployment

We deploy LaViRA-M³ on a heterogeneous team of three robots in an office — an Agilex Cobot Magic wheeled platform, a Unitree Go1, and a Unitree Go2 quadruped. Each robot carries an RGB-D camera, a Livox Mid-360 LiDAR for localization and mapping, and an onboard computer (an RTX 4090 on the Cobot Magic, a Jetson Orin NX on each quadruped) running its own LaViRA agent with a locally deployed Qwen3.8-27B in 4-bit GGUF quantization — no cloud API. The robots exchange the shared map as voxel records over Wi-Fi, together with the goal board and their progress analysis, through the same channels as in simulation.
In the shown run, the team handles three goals at once — an image of a yellow suitcase, the category "printer", and the description "green chair besides the coffee bar" — the robots spread out through the office, each claims the goal it encounters, and a goal found by one robot is removed from the others' lists. The shared map grows over time, colored by robot, with regions mapped by more than one robot in pink.
